25 Fitness Challenge Ideas for Friends (With Rules You Can Copy)

September 27, 2026 · 8 min read

Short answer: The best fitness challenges for friends are short (one to four weeks), tracked automatically, and scored in a way that’s fair across fitness levels. Start with a one-week step challenge, then rotate formats: distance, active minutes, streaks, duels and team goals. All 25 ideas below come with rules, a scoring method and a suggested length.

Most fitness challenges between friends start the same way. Someone posts “step challenge this week?” in the group chat, eight people reply with fire emojis, and by Thursday two of them are still posting screenshots. The idea was fine. The format wasn’t.

Here are 25 challenge ideas grouped by type, with the details that decide whether they last: how to score it, how long to run it, and who it suits.

Why a little competition works

Friendly competition works better than you might expect, and better than encouragement on its own:

  • In the STEP UP trial, adults in a competitive step game walked about 920 more steps a day than the control group. The competitive group was the only one still ahead three months after the game ended.
  • In a 790-person trial, groups that could compare themselves with others attended about 90% more exercise classes than groups that couldn’t. The purely supportive group did worst of all.
  • Exercise spreads between friends. Across 1.1 million runners, each extra kilometre a person’s friends ran inspired them to run about 0.3 km more.

Support still matters, but on its own it doesn’t get people moving. What moves people is seeing where they stand next to people they know.

Step and walking challenges

  1. The classic step week. Most total steps from Monday to Sunday. Length: 7 days. Best for: first challenges, mixed groups.
  2. Beat your baseline. Everyone’s goal is their own two-week average plus 15%. Most days over goal wins. Length: 2 weeks. Best for: groups where one person walks 20,000 a day and another 4,000.
  3. The weekend warrior. Most steps between Saturday morning and Sunday night. It’s short, intense and good for busy weeks. Length: 2 days.
  4. 10K club. One point for every day over 10,000 steps. Highest points total wins. Length: 2–4 weeks.
  5. Lunch-break loop. Everyone does a 15-minute walk between 12 and 2 every weekday. Anyone who misses a day is out; last one standing wins. Length: 2 weeks.
  6. Virtual journey. Add up the group’s steps (about 1,300 steps per km at an average walking stride) to “walk” a real route together: across your city, or to a friend’s home town. Length: 1 month.

Running and distance challenges

  1. Distance race. Most kilometres run, walked or cycled in a week. Set a conversion if people use different sports (for example, 3 km on the bike counts as 1 km on foot). Length: 1 week.
  2. Handicap 5K. Everyone runs 5K. Your score is how much you beat your own previous best by, not your finishing time. Length: 1 day, repeated monthly.
  3. Monthly 50. Everyone who reaches 50 km in a month is in; the others buy the post-run breakfast. Length: 1 month.
  4. Longest single effort. Longest one-off run or walk of the week wins. It’s good for building up to a first 10K. Length: 1 week.
  5. Hill of the month. Most elevation gained. It suits trail runners and cyclists and gives flat-city runners a reason to find the one hill in town. Length: 1 month.

Consistency and streak challenges

  1. Never miss twice. Any workout counts. Missing one day is fine; missing two in a row knocks you out. Length: 30 days.
  2. Active-minutes race. Most workout minutes in a week, any sport. The fairest format for groups doing different things. Length: 1 week.
  3. Three-a-week. Everyone commits to three workouts a week. Hit it and you score; miss it and you owe the group a coffee. Length: 4–8 weeks.
  4. The 21-day streak. A workout of at least 20 minutes every day for 21 days, with one freeze allowed. Length: 21 days.
  5. Early-bird week. Workouts only count if they start before 8 am. Most sessions wins. Length: 1 week.
  6. The variety pack. One point per different type of workout completed in a month: run, swim, yoga, climbing, football, strength. Length: 1 month.

Strength and bodyweight challenges

  1. Push-up ladder. Day 1: 5 push-ups. Add 1 every day. First to fail a day is out. Knee push-ups count if agreed beforehand. Length: until one person is left.
  2. Plank personal best. Beat your own plank time by 5 seconds every day. Scored against yourself, so fitness level doesn’t matter. Length: 2 weeks.
  3. 100 squats a day. Split them however you like across the day. A simple yes/no each day, with most days completed winning. Length: 2 weeks.
  4. Strength sessions count. One point per strength workout of 30+ minutes, capped at four a week. It balances out groups where only the runners ever win. Length: 1 month.

Team and co-op challenges

  1. Team vs team. Split into two teams and compare total workout minutes. The pressure of letting your team down is stronger than any prize. Length: 2 weeks.
  2. Group goal. The whole group works towards one number, such as 1,000 km in a month. Everyone wins or nobody does. Length: 1 month.
  3. Duel ladder. Everyone is ranked. Challenge anyone up to two places above you to a one-on-one duel; win and you swap places. Length: ongoing.
  4. Relay week. Each person “owns” one day and has to beat the previous day’s distance. The group score is how many days in a row the chain holds. Length: 1 week.

Rules to agree on before day one

  • What counts: only automatically tracked activity, or manual entries too?
  • Start and end: exact dates and times, and which time zone if you’re spread out.
  • Sick days: can someone pause, or use one freeze?
  • The prize: small and social. A coffee, a brunch, bragging rights.
  • What’s next: decide the next challenge before this one ends, so there’s no gap.

The biggest single fix is the first one. Challenges that rely on people reporting their own numbers tend to fade by the second week. We explain why in why most group fitness challenges fail by day 3.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good fitness challenge to do with friends?

A one-week step challenge is the best starting point. Anyone with a phone can take part, it’s fair across fitness levels, it’s tracked automatically, and a week is short enough that everyone stays interested. Once that works, try distance, minutes or streak formats.

How long should a fitness challenge with friends last?

One to four weeks. One week is best for a first challenge. For anything longer, split it into weekly rounds with a winner each week so people who fall behind still have something to play for.

How do you keep a friends fitness challenge fair across fitness levels?

Score against each person’s own baseline (for example, percentage of their own average steps), use points for consistency rather than raw totals, or give a handicap. Absolute totals only work when everyone trains at a similar level.

Can you do a fitness challenge with friends who live in other cities?

Yes. Step, distance and active-minute challenges work anywhere, as long as everyone’s activity is tracked automatically by their phone or watch and shown on one shared leaderboard.

What’s a good prize for a fitness challenge between friends?

Small and social works best: the loser buys the post-challenge coffee or brunch, the winner picks the next challenge, or everyone chips in a small amount for a group activity. Big cash prizes tend to invite arguments about the rules.
