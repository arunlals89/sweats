12 Workplace Fitness Challenge Ideas Your Team Will Actually Join

September 27, 2026 · 8 min read

A good workplace fitness challenge gets people talking at lunch, mixes teams that don’t usually work together, and gets people moving more. A bad one gets a burst of sign-ups on Monday, three active people by Wednesday, and a quiet channel by week two. The difference is rarely the idea. It’s whether the challenge works for everyone, from the marathon runner to the person who hasn’t exercised in years.

Here are 12 workplace fitness challenge ideas that work for mixed fitness levels, followed by the setup rules that keep them going.

Step-based challenges

1. Team step total

Split into teams of four to six and add up every member’s steps for the week. Because it’s a team total, every step counts, so the least active person still helps and nobody gets left behind. This is the best first challenge for most offices.

2. Personal-best step ramp

Everyone measures their own baseline, then tries to beat it by a set percentage each week. The winner is whoever improves most, not whoever walks most, so it’s fair for everyone. Our 30-day step challenge plan works well as the template.

3. Walking meeting week

For one week, every 1:1 that doesn’t need a screen becomes a walking meeting. Track how many meetings each team moves outside. It’s low effort, costs nothing and gets steps into the working day itself.

Team and league challenges

4. Department league

Departments earn points for any activity, whether walking, cycling, gym or yoga, and a weekly table shows who’s on top. Reset it every Monday so a slow week doesn’t knock a team out of the whole thing. That’s the same idea behind weekly fitness leagues.

5. Virtual relay across a map

Pick a well-known route (a famous coastline, a road trip, the distance between two office locations) and see how quickly the whole company can cover it together. Post progress on a map each week. It works for any sport that logs distance.

6. Mixed-team bingo

Create a bingo card of activities, such as a 20-minute walk, a yoga class, taking the stairs all day, a bike commute or trying a new sport. Mixed teams from different departments race to complete a line. It’s great for variety and meeting new colleagues.

Habit and consistency challenges

7. Active-minutes streak

Everyone aims for at least 20 active minutes a day. Allow one or two “freeze” days a month so a sick day doesn’t reset someone to zero. It’s one of the easiest ways to stop people dropping out (here’s why freezes work).

8. The 3-a-week club

The goal is simple: three workouts of any kind, every week, for a month. Everyone who makes it gets the same reward. There’s no leaderboard at the top, just a finish line anyone can reach.

9. Commute challenge

Points for any part of the commute done on foot or by bike. Encourage partial commutes, like cycling to the station or walking the last kilometre, so people who live far away can still take part.

Quick, fun challenges

10. Lunchtime duels

Anyone can challenge a colleague to a head-to-head, such as most steps by 5pm or furthest walk this lunch break. It’s spontaneous, friendly, and a great way to get the competitive people excited without making it the whole company’s focus. See how 1-on-1 duels work.

11. Stair-climb week

If your building has stairs, count flights climbed for a week. Add a “floors of a famous building” goal to make it concrete.

12. Try-something-new month

Each week, everyone tries a different activity (climbing, swimming, a dance class, a hike) and shares a photo. Nobody wins on performance. The reward is variety, and some people find a new habit.

How to run a workplace challenge that doesn’t fizzle

Most office challenges fade for the same few reasons, which we cover in detail in why most group fitness challenges fail by day 3. Here’s the short version:

  • Track automatically. Manual logging, screenshots and shared spreadsheets are the first things people stop doing. Use an app that reads activity from people’s phones or watches so the leaderboard fills itself.
  • Reward participation, not just the top spot. If only the fittest person can win, everyone else stops trying by day three. Use team totals, improvement-based scoring or completion rewards.
  • Keep rounds short. Two to four weeks is plenty. Weekly resets give people who fall behind a fresh start.
  • Make progress visible. A live leaderboard and a short weekly shout-out in the team channel keep it in people’s minds.
  • Make it inclusive. Count many types of activity (not just running), offer low-impact options, and never share anyone’s individual numbers without their consent.
  • Have one organiser, not a committee. One enthusiastic person posting weekly updates keeps things going better than a planning group.

A simple four-week rollout

  1. Week 0: Announce the challenge, form mixed teams and get everyone set up on the same tracking app.
  2. Week 1: Start with a team step total (idea #1) to get everyone involved.
  3. Week 2: Add lunchtime duels (idea #10) for extra energy.
  4. Week 3: Switch to an active-minutes goal so non-walkers can shine.
  5. Week 4: Finish with a company-wide relay total and a small celebration.

After that, ask people which part they enjoyed most and build the next round around it.
