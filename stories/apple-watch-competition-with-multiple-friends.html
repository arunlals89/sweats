How to Do an Apple Watch Competition With Multiple Friends

September 27, 2026 · 8 min read

Short answer: You can’t. Every Apple Watch competition is between exactly two people. It lasts seven days, and you score one point per percent of your rings closed, capped at 600 a day. To compete as a group, either run a separate one-on-one competition with each friend (a “round robin”), or use an app that reads Apple Health and puts everyone on one leaderboard.

Apple’s Activity competitions are one of the best things about the Apple Watch. A week-long race, a notification every time your friend pulls ahead, a trophy at the end. The problem comes when you try to invite your whole friend group, family or office team. There’s no “add another person” button, and there never has been. The top-voted answer on Apple’s own forum puts it simply: “you can challenge only one friend at a time to a competition.”

This guide covers exactly how Apple’s scoring works (it matters for fairness), the workaround most groups try first, and how to set up a single group leaderboard that still uses your Apple Watch data.

How Apple Watch competitions work

Here are the rules as Apple documents them. You can check them in Apple’s Activity sharing guide.

RuleWhat it means
PlayersTwo people per competition, both already sharing Activity with each other
Length7 days, once your friend accepts
Scoring1 point for every percent you add to your rings each day
Daily cap600 points a day
Maximum score4,200 points for the week
WinnerWhoever has the most points after seven days
Sharing limitUp to 40 friends

How to start one

  1. Open the Activity app on your Apple Watch.
  2. Tap the sharing button and choose a friend you already share Activity with.
  3. Scroll down and tap Invite [name].
  4. Wait for them to accept. The seven days start once they do.

Why the points system is less fair than it looks

Because points are a percentage of your own goals, two people can do very different amounts of work and score the same. If your Move goal is 800 active calories and your friend’s is 300, closing your ring and closing theirs are both worth 100 points, even though you burned more than twice as much.

That’s deliberate. It lets people at different fitness levels compete. But it has two side effects that come up again and again in group chats:

  • Goal setting becomes strategy. Whoever has the easiest goals has the easiest week. Ring goals are set by each person, so a lower Move goal is the fastest route to a higher score.
  • The cap flattens big days. Once you hit 600 points in a day, nothing more counts. A long weekend ride scores the same as an average day where you comfortably closed everything.

Neither is a problem between two friends who trust each other. Across a group of eight with very different routines, it’s the main reason competitions stop feeling fair.

Workaround 1: the round robin (everyone challenges everyone)

You can run separate one-on-one competitions with several friends at the same time. So the closest you can get to a group competition inside Apple’s system is for every person to challenge every other person. The number of competitions grows quickly:

Group sizeCompetitions neededEach person runs
3 friends32 at once
4 friends63 at once
6 friends155 at once
10 friends459 at once

It works for three or four people. Beyond that, the problems pile up:

  • There’s no single leaderboard. You see your own head-to-heads, not who’s actually winning the group.
  • Start dates drift. Each competition starts when its invite is accepted, so half the group finishes on Tuesday and half on Thursday.
  • It stops after seven days. Every competition has to be started again by hand, and after two or three weeks someone always forgets.

Workaround 2: screenshots in the group chat

The other common fix is a WhatsApp group where everyone posts their rings or step count each evening and one person keeps a spreadsheet. It works for about four days. After that, the scorekeeper gets busy, screenshots arrive late, somebody’s number gets disputed, and the challenge quietly dies. We wrote about that pattern in detail in why most group fitness challenges fail by day 3. The short version: if scoring depends on people reporting their own numbers, it eventually stops.

Workaround 3: one leaderboard that reads Apple Health

Your Apple Watch writes its steps, walking and running distance, active calories and workouts into Apple Health on your iPhone. Any app that can read Apple Health can put everyone’s numbers on one shared leaderboard automatically, with nothing to report and no screenshots.

When you compare options, check four things:

  1. It reads Apple Health automatically. Nobody should have to log anything by hand.
  2. It scores an absolute metric. Steps, distance or active minutes are the same for everyone, so nobody can win by setting easier goals.
  3. You set the dates. A weekend, a week, a month, with one start line for the whole group.
  4. It includes friends without a watch. An iPhone counts steps on its own, so step and distance challenges can include them.

A little competition is worth setting up properly. In the STEP UP trial (602 adults, 24 weeks), the group that competed on step goals averaged about 920 more steps a day than the control group. It was also the only group that stayed ahead after the game was switched off.

Which metric to use for a mixed group

MetricBest forWatch out for
StepsMixed fitness levels, walkers, friends without a watchUndercounts cycling, swimming and gym sessions
DistanceGroups of runners and walkersFavours people with long commutes on foot
Workout minutesGroups doing different sports (gym, yoga, football, runs)Only counts recorded workouts, not general movement
Active caloriesGroups of similar size and fitnessEstimates vary by device and body size, so bigger people score more

If you’re unsure, pick steps for a week. It’s the fairest option for most groups, and it works for anyone with a phone.

  • Metric: total steps, Monday to Sunday.

Frequently asked questions

Can you compete with more than one person on Apple Watch?

Not in a single competition. Apple Watch competitions are one-on-one. You can run separate one-on-one competitions with several friends at the same time, but there’s no built-in group leaderboard.

How are Apple Watch competition points calculated?

You earn one point for every percent you add to your Activity rings each day, up to 600 points a day. A competition lasts seven days, so the maximum is 4,200 points, and whoever has the most points at the end wins.

How many friends can you share Activity with?

Up to 40. Sharing lets you see each other’s rings and workouts, but each competition is still between just two people.

Is an Apple Watch competition fair between people with different goals?

Only partly. Points are a percentage of each person’s own ring goals, so someone with a lower Move goal can close rings more easily. For a mixed group, a shared absolute metric like steps or active minutes is fairer.

Can someone without an Apple Watch join?

Not in Apple’s own competitions, which are built around Activity rings. An iPhone still counts steps and walking distance in Apple Health, so a step or distance challenge in an app that reads Apple Health can include them.
